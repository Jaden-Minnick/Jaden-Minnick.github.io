Where it began

“Fusion is the Future.”

My interest in nuclear energy began in high school, in a manufacturing certification program supported through the National Science Foundation, the University of Virginia, and my local community college. We studied Lean manufacturing, Six Sigma, just-in-time production, and Kaizen. But what stayed with me most was our instructor, Mr. Jim Leech.

After more than 18 years as an engineer at Hershey and a career in industrial engineering consulting, Mr. Leech challenged us to think beyond the factory floor. He asked us to consider climate change, energy affordability, hunger, and other problems his generation was handing to ours—not as reasons to give up, but as challenges we could help solve.

We often talked about energy, and he would tell me, “Fusion is the Future.” Those conversations sparked my fascination with nuclear fusion and, eventually, with the broader potential of fission, fusion, and integrated nuclear-energy systems. Today, I want to understand how advanced nuclear technologies can be made safer, more resource-efficient, economically viable, and useful to more people.

Featured Ph.D. research · Current

Uranium chloride molten salts for fission

My present graduate research focuses on uranium chloride molten salt chemistry relevant to advanced fission reactors. I am interested in the local atomic environments and chemical interactions that influence how these complex salts behave under reactor-relevant conditions.

This is one part of a broader research vision: developing the chemical understanding and engineering tools needed for advanced fission systems, sustainable fuel cycles, and future fusion applications.
