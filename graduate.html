Primary Ph.D. research · Current

Uranium chloride fuel-salt chemistry

Investigating how uranium is coordinated within chloride molten salts and how structural information can help describe complex fission-reactor fuel chemistry.

Explore research questions and methods +

Questions: How does the local uranium–chloride environment change with salt composition or temperature? How can we interpret measured structural signatures in molten systems?

Approaches: High-temperature salt chemistry, X-ray absorption spectroscopy, scattering data, and atomistic modeling methods, including reverse Monte Carlo analysis.

Research methods · Developing expertise

X-ray spectroscopy and atomistic modeling

Using experimental data alongside computational approaches to build a clearer picture of local atomic environments.

What do these techniques reveal? +

XANES: Information about electronic structure and oxidation-state-sensitive spectral features.

EXAFS: Local coordination shells, interatomic distances, and disorder around an absorbing atom.

Total scattering and PDF: Real-space patterns of atomic pair distances and local disorder, particularly useful for disordered materials.

RMC: Atomistic configurations adjusted to agree with measured scattering information, subject to structural constraints.

Complementary research

Rare earth recovery and separation chemistry

My broader graduate interests include chemical separations and recovering critical materials used in advanced technologies.

Why this matters +

Selective recovery and recycling can reduce reliance on primary extraction and create opportunities for more resource-efficient industrial processes. These projects are distinct from my uranium chloride Ph.D. focus.

Future research direction

Molten salts for fusion energy

I am interested in the role molten salts may play in some fusion blanket concepts, including materials compatibility, chemical control, and fuel-cycle-relevant processes.

How this connects to my long-term goals +

Fusion presents different technical challenges than fission. My aim is to develop transferable expertise in molten salt chemistry and apply it where it can support safe and sustainable nuclear energy technologies.

Proposed laboratory automation project · Fall 2026

Automated Chemical Inventory Management System

I proposed a low-cost, fixed laboratory station to make chemical inventory tracking safer, faster, and easier for researchers in ZEC 212.

Explore the proposed system and evaluation plan +

System concept: A precision electronic balance, barcode scanner, thermal label printer, Raspberry Pi, and touchscreen, with a Python interface and searchable inventory database.

Pilot goals: Evaluate inventory accuracy, transaction success, and chemical retrieval times against current laboratory workflows. Proposed targets include 95% inventory agreement, 95% transaction success, and a 30% reduction in retrieval time.

Status: Research award proposal and planned pilot. Results and deployment will be added when available.
